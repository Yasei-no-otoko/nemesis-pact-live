## Project description

NEMESIS PACT is a browser roguelite arena shooter where negotiating with an enemy changes the rules you must survive. Players speak or type to THE NOTARY, revise proposals, inspect a Living Covenant, and explicitly sign before clauses affect combat. Beyond First Contact, six sectors offer three airframes, branching routes, upgrades, relics, and distinct bosses. GPT-5.6 Luna can analyze aggregate wave or sector telemetry at encounter boundaries and return one bounded ease, hold, or raise suggestion. Authored modifiers change bullet speed and arrival spacing by at most 16 percent; canonical pacts remain unchanged. The campaign uses fixed autoplay input for repeatability, not an AI pilot. Deterministic WebGL2 and WebGPU rendering, synthesized music, captions, touch controls, and explicit LOCAL RULES fallback keep the experience playable without a microphone or API access. The production game, source, and existing human gameplay video are publicly accessible for review.

## Judging criteria 1 (30%) - Meaningful use of OpenAI tools

GPT-Live-1 makes spoken negotiation a playable control surface. Over Live API and WebRTC, players speak to THE NOTARY, interrupt its answer, and revise terms in English or Japanese. Typed requests enter the same contract pipeline. Luna proposes combinations of authored clauses; validation rejects unsupported mechanics, and only the player's Sign action applies a displayed contract. Generated speech cannot grant powers or silently change combat. Revision tracking invalidates superseded proposals. For expedition mode, Luna analyzes bounded aggregate telemetry at wave or sector completion. The next encounter can apply one authored difficulty step, with bullet speed and arrival spacing capped at plus or minus 16 percent. Canonical pacts remain unchanged. If analysis is unavailable, deterministic LOCAL RULES hold the current level. The campaign autoplay input is fixed; it is not an AI pilot. Codex supported implementation and verification. The meaningful contribution is a continuous loop from natural language intention to understandable combat rules while deterministic simulation preserves responsive reflex play.

## Judging criteria 2 (25%) - Originality

NEMESIS PACT turns a boss encounter into a negotiated ruleset. The opponent is an adversary and the counterparty to a Living Covenant. Conversation creates choices the player must execute: protecting one flank may introduce hostile reinforcements, while stronger reflected damage can require weaker ordinary shots. The enemy's offer becomes a strategic build decision with consequences. Its distinctive interaction is revision under pressure. A player can interrupt the speaking rival, change a request, review corrected clauses, and sign. During battle, a second negotiation changes tactics without erasing accumulated damage or elapsed time. The system gives natural language flexibility a legible mechanical boundary: every advantage and price is inspectable, unsupported promises are rejected, and the final encounter record reports actual effects. A bounded adaptive analysis extends authorship into the six-sector campaign by using aggregate wave or sector results to suggest one authored difficulty step at the next boundary. Fixed autoplay makes that demonstration repeatable without making the model an AI pilot. The combination connects conversational AI, negotiated commitments, and a precision two dimensional shooter through one coherent fantasy: write the terms, then prove you can survive them.

## Judging criteria 3 (25%) - Playability / Utility

The production HTTPS game is playable in a desktop or mobile browser. First Contact presents a loop: negotiate, inspect benefits and costs, sign, fight, renegotiate once, and defeat the boss. Automatic aim and fire in this mode let new players focus on movement, parrying, dashing, and understanding their contract. Players can type or use GPT-Live-1 voice. Captions, output volume, mute, transcripts, and consent controls support accessibility. Microphone refusal, connection failure, or exhausted API allowance produces an explicit LOCAL RULES alternative. The full campaign adds six sectors, six bosses, three airframes, route choices, upgrades, relics, and adaptive encounter analysis. Wave and sector results are reviewed at boundaries before authored bullet speed or arrival spacing changes by at most 16 percent. Keyboard controls and portrait touch controls support play contexts. Existing human iPhone footage demonstrates interruption, corrected contracts, touch combat, renegotiation, and victory. A new submission movie will explicitly label automated piloting and synthetic player speech, and identify actual GPT-Live-1 and Luna use from the capture. Fixed autoplay input remains clearly distinct from an AI pilot.

## Judging criteria 4 (20%) - Execution and craft

The game combines deterministic two dimensional combat with a custom WebGL2 and WebGPU renderer, without Three.js or a game framework. Procedural geometry, shaders, and synthesized Web Audio deliver self contained HTML with no imported models, font downloads, or sampled soundtrack assets. OpenAI generated concept boards guided visual refinement: the geometric Notary, color hierarchy, sanctuaries, contract review, and engraved encounter records. The implementation separates AI analysis from deterministic authority. Signed sessions, CSRF checks, durable Redis quotas, atomic budget reservations, concurrency limits, and a kill switch protect paid endpoints. Adaptive decisions are bounded, deduplicated by run and sequence, and applied only at encounter boundaries. Existing human gameplay footage was edited in DaVinci Resolve to 59 seconds at 1080p60, retaining original voices and documenting capture limitations. New automated footage will label fixed autoplay and synthetic player speech clearly. This description reports the implemented design and existing human footage without claiming a completed full live adaptive run or capture.
